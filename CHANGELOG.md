## [Unreleased]

### Since 2026-09-07 (build-plan items 17-39 and the fixes around them)

#### Added

- **Authentication and onboarding release hardening (item 19)** - the complete web and mobile entry lifecycle was rebuilt and qualified across roles, browsers, and devices. Admission waits for the effective organization's bootstrap so a completed member is never returned to onboarding by a refresh, token rotation, realtime org change, or role change (`organization_memberships.onboarding_completed_at` is authoritative). Invitation acceptance, organization entry, sign-in, session restoration, expiry and revocation, password recovery, MFA enrollment and challenge, and the role-based setup, trial, and billing gates each have live evidence (`e2e/auth-release-qualification.spec.ts`, `lib/auth/release-qualification.ts`). Cold and warm entry are measured (`npm run measure:auth:web`, `measure:auth:mobile`), avoidable serial work is gone, and degraded networks get bounded retries with an explicit recovery surface instead of a blank screen (`@dubgrid/client-errors` owns the retry policy and honors `Retry-After`). Cross-tab auth, token rotation, foreground resume, and organization switching keep identity, permissions, and tenant data consistent.
- **Sensitive-action reauthentication** - a five-minute fresh-auth window derived from the JWT `amr` timestamps (`@dubgrid/authz` `assurance.ts`, `requireSensitiveActionAuth`). MFA changes, credential updates, other-session revocation, data export, and account or organization deletion require fresh AAL2 proof when a verified TOTP factor exists, otherwise a fresh password proof, on web (`StepUpDialog`) and mobile. One shared MFA lifecycle handler (`POST /api/account/mfa-lifecycle`, `POST /api/mobile/v1/profile/mfa-lifecycle`) runs `enroll`, `remove`, `reauthenticate`, and `cleanup` over user-scoped provider calls; `POST /api/account/credential-assurance` preflights Supabase `updateUser`. The provider-boundary qualification and its accepted limits are recorded in `docs/mfa-provider-boundary.md`.
- **Live tenant authorization** - one contract for web cookies, web bearer, and mobile bearer: a signature-verified, non-revoked session plus current live membership in exactly one organization. Migration `016` cuts direct user-scoped queries off when the tracked session row is removed and `017` binds effective-tenant state to the auth session. (The regular-user visibility repairs that followed are listed under Changed below.)
- **Invite, recovery, redirect, and CSRF integrity** - invitation and recovery credentials expire, bind to the intended identity, and are single-use (`accept_invitation` row-locked in migration `018`; access-level changes replace the token atomically via `012`). Password recovery is requested through `POST /api/auth/recovery-request` (mobile: `/api/mobile/v1/auth/recovery-request`) with source, target, and global limits and a security audit event. Post-auth redirects are pinned by `lib/auth/integrity-contract.ts`, and `__tests__/auth-integrity-entry-points.test.ts` inventories every mutating route so a browser-facing mutation cannot ship without `validateCsrfOrigin`.
- **Abuse resistance and auditability** - generic public identity-facing failures; login limited per email, per source IP, and globally (`loginIpLimiter`, `loginSurgeLimiter`, env-tunable) and failing closed in production; credentials kept out of URLs and telemetry (`lib/auth/security-redaction.ts`); security outcomes written through `lib/auth/security-audit.ts` without secrets.
- **Scheduler open-shift staffing (item 22)** - clicking an open shift shows active staff who satisfy its focus-area, role, and certification requirements and have no absence or overlapping shift that day, including adjacent-day overnight work; the scheduler is identified as "You", search never strands a hidden selection, and the assignment becomes an ordinary draft change that reaches staff through publish. An adjacent day outside the loaded window is reported as "can't verify" rather than a false negative (item 32). The `finalize_scheduler_staffed_calloffs` trigger (migration `019`, repaired in `020`) approves a call-off-backed pickup when the scheduler's matching draft is published; item 33 smoke-tested it against real data.
- **Explicit indicator removal (item 20)** - an active shift indicator in the web shift slideover has a plainly named removal action.
- **Calendar subscription** - a private `.ics` feed per employee (`/api/calendar/feed/[token]`, `calendar_feed_tokens`, migration `011`), issued, rotated, and revoked from the profile's `CalendarSubscriptionCard` through `/api/account/calendar-subscription`; changes are confirmed in-app, never with a native dialog.
- **Date-navigated activity everywhere** - the organization Activity Log, the per-person Activity page, and the Gridmaster audit log share one period navigator, table, and stats, backed by an audience-scoped audit registry (`lib/audit/`) and a per-day count endpoint.
- **Mobile dashboard (items 18b, 18c, 38b)** - `/api/mobile/v1/dashboard` delivers the canonical coverage, open-shift, pending-approval, staff-hours, and unpublished-draft facts from the shared `schedule-core` engine; the admin Home tab is one designed surface with a coverage hero on the brand aurora (amber and red carry news, blue means normal), tappable drill-in rows, header-right "See all", and skeletons that mirror the real screens. Period-history trends were built and then removed.
- **Mobile role certification eligibility (item 18a)** - role credential requirements reach mobile and incompatible new selections are blocked before save.
- **Mobile design contract (item 38)** - one spacing ramp (4-48), a `display`/`screenTitle`/`title`/`sectionTitle` type ramp, a 36/44/52 control scale, fill-only badges, and a `design/no-raw-mobile-metrics` lint error; every raw font size and off-ramp spacing literal was migrated (item 38d). Request flows exit on one tap: swap target selection on a full-page sheet, a confirmation for call-off only (38c). The confirmation popup, keyboard accessory bar, and tab strip moved onto the scale (38e). A screenshot-qualified role, theme, text-scale, and device matrix lives under `blueprint/reference/mobile/` with `scripts/mobile-shot.sh` and `scripts/mobile-matrix.sh` (38f). Choice groups are grouped lists (`ProfileChoiceGroup`), not chip clouds or dropdowns.
- **App-wide pill overflow resilience (item 23)** - pill, chip, tag, badge, and segmented-choice text stays inside its bounds on web and mobile: display values may wrap and grow, interactive controls stay one line and truncate while keeping the full accessible value. The shared count badge that came out of it (item 23a) is listed under Added below.
- **Inter product typography (item 24)** - Inter for all product UI and ordinary copy (Inter Variable with optical sizing on web, native 400/500/600/700 faces on mobile), DM Sans kept for the wordmark and landing headings, tabular numerals on scheduling figures (`dg-tabular-nums`, `mobileTabularText`).
- **Web UI consistency (item 25)** - semantic statuses, switches, numeric counts, and auth action labels consolidated onto shared primitives (`StatusPill`, `Switch`, `NumericBadge`); `useMediaQuery` no longer paints the wide-desktop layout for a frame on phones, tablets, and zoomed desktops; the tablet dashboard, the Alerts and Schedule toolbars at high zoom, and the schedule grid's totals-row border junction were repaired. Every core route's loading, error, empty, dialog, trial, and overlay state, every role including Gridmaster impersonation, and the Gridmaster portal itself are qualified with live Playwright evidence (`e2e/*-states.spec.ts`, `role-variance*.spec.ts`), with `qa-admin` and `qa-gridmaster` fixtures added to the seed.
- **Mobile app icons and splash** - the app icon is the DM Sans wordmark stacked as dub over grid on brand blue (`npm run icons`), safe inside both platforms' masks; the OS launch image is the first frame of the JS splash so the handoff is seamless; one `StartupSplashGate` above the router owns the splash.
- **Migration readiness and production runbook** - `npm run db:migrations:check` locks the ordered migration stream against `checksums.sha256`; `npm run db:migrations:inspect` (and `:local`) is a read-only ledger, schema, RLS, grant, hook, historical-patch, and scheduler-calloff qualification with an HTTPS Management API fallback transport; `docs/operations/production-migration-safety.md` is the release gate. `db:reset:remote` refuses the production ref, `db:reset:staging` and `db:seed:branch` cover staging and preview branches, and `npm run auth:templates:push` syncs the compiled auth emails to the linked project.
- **Git hooks** - `npm run hooks:install` enables `.githooks/`: pre-commit Prettier and ESLint over staged files, `commit-msg` stripping AI attribution trailers, pre-push type-check and tests through Turborepo.

#### Changed

- **Mobile onboarding previews** - the first-run tour's two mock-ups are live renders of the Home hero card and the Requests Available list (`features/onboarding/components/previews`), drawn with the app's own components, theme, and text size from sample data and today's date, in place of PNG captures that dated as soon as the screens changed.
- **Cell locking replaced by editor presence** - advisory cell leases and their trigger-driven broadcasts stopped delivering after a few hops and stranded editors behind takeover dialogs. `useSchedulePresence` now publishes identity once and paces an `editing_cell` broadcast; peers see a non-blocking marker, positions expire on their own, and the optimistic version check remains the data-safety boundary. Editor display details are fetched on demand (`/api/schedule/presence-profiles`) rather than broadcast, persistent same-account sessions are called out, and editor sessions can be ended explicitly (`/api/schedule/editor-sessions`, migration `013`).
- **Migrations are an immutable ordered stream** - `001`-`004` are a frozen baseline and every later schema change is a new, retry-safe numbered file; the old rule to mirror changes back into the canonical files was retired after five migrations drifted, and `supabase/patches/` is evidence only. Publish history is a management-only read, and a schedule note cannot exist without a shift on its cell (migration `015`).
- **Management access is one popup** - on web and mobile the management editor carries departments only; roles change through the header select, the Access card, the mobile badge, or the roster's Change Access Level. Slide-over panels share one implementation and the shift editor opens faster. Peer action groups follow secondary-first / primary-last in every layout, and confirmation dialogs put Cancel left and the action right with dismissal blocked while pending.
- **Busy buttons** - an async action button latches with `useAsyncAction` and shows a spinner in place of its leading icon with the label unchanged; the `loadingLabel` prop is gone.
- **Dashboard and staff pages stop double-loading** - the People page, the schedule's published-range fetch, the dashboard's shift-request fetch and its private realtime channel, and the Reports page's options-only fetch each make one request where they made two (items 26, 28, 29, 30); mobile shift-request screens share one reference-counted realtime channel per organization (item 35).
- **Settings tables** - every Settings data table starts from the Departments width, grows for edit or dense modes, and shows complete field values; role names render as plain text; the display-mode preview stays fully visible and selectable at supported widths and zoom levels (item 21). `/settings/staff-config` now only redirects into `/settings`.
- **Dashboards offer Week and 2 Weeks** - the Day period is gone from the web and mobile dashboards, along with its own alignment, labels and jump-to-today; the web schedule strip shows the selected period as is.
- **Request cards say what they are and who asked** - on web and mobile a request card carries one pill that names its kind (`Swap request`, `Pickup request`, `Time off request`, beside the open-shift card's `Open shift`) and folds the status in once it is decided (`Swap · Pending approval`, `Swap · Approved`); the requester heads the card, a sentence says whom they asked (`Asked Laura Marshall to swap shifts`, with "you" for the viewer), and a swap holds both people's shifts as two panels joined by the swap arrow, each labelled `Laura's shift` / `Your shift`. Shift, focus area, day and time read as text rather than pills, and the shared copy lives in `@dubgrid/domain` (`shift-request-copy.ts`). Decline, Reject and Cancel are neutral buttons in a secondary-left / primary-right grid, never red; Volunteer loses its icon and fills its row. Approvers can leave an optional note with either decision (`Note to Brian and Laura? (Optional)`), which the decision notification carries to both people. The web request board opens at its usual width and grows leftwards as the active tab needs columns, and managers get a History tab with a shift-date range picker, type, outcome and a pick-many staff filter. The onboarding tour's request slide is redrawn from the same card.
- **Mobile dashboard period switches are instant** - both periods load together from the first paint and stay equally fresh, so the toggle serves the other one from cache; when one still has to load, a full-screen scrim with a centered spinner covers the wait instead of dimming the cards. In 2 Weeks the coverage strip pages a week at a time with a dot indicator, Your schedule shows one week or two with the toggle, and tapping a day in it opens that shift's or absence's detail page.
- **Mobile people are added the same way** - one Add button on the People tab asks whether the person is scheduled staff or management staff, as web's Add menu does, then opens that form as a sheet; the pushed `/people/add` page is gone. The management invitation picks the access level from the same described radio list as the App access sheet (Super Admin offered only by a Super Admin, as on web) and its departments from the ring-and-check list the management-access sheet already used; the last two chip clouds (management user access, call-off absence reason) became lists too.
- **Manager cancel for requests awaiting a recipient** - a swap, or a pickup offered to one person, cannot be decided until that person answers, so it sat in the manager's queue on web with no note and no action, and off the mobile Approval tab entirely. Both now show it under "Swaps awaiting a response" with "Waiting for {name} to respond" and a Cancel request action whose confirmation says the recipient has not answered yet and that the schedule stays as it is (`isAwaitingRecipient`, `describeCancelAwaitingRecipientCaution` in `@dubgrid/domain`). The web approval queue and the mobile Approval tab are grouped by what each request waits on: Pending approval, Pickups, Swaps awaiting a response (`groupManagerQueue`).
- **Request date ranges apply on a button** - the requests History tab and the Reports custom range use one `DateRangePicker` built on the shadcn `Calendar` (`react-day-picker`, two months side by side): two taps draft a range and Apply commits it, so the list no longer refetches on the first tap with a half-made range; closing any other way discards the draft, and the trigger reads both ends the same way (`Sep 6 - Sep 26, 2026`). The hand-rolled range mode of `calendar-date-picker` and the Reports page's private calendar grid are gone.
- **Toolbars and fields are content-sized** - every web toolbar and filter row is a wrapping row in which each control is exactly as wide as its own value (Reports, the requests History tab, Alerts, People, Activity, Coverage, the Gridmaster views), so nothing truncates while a neighbour sits half empty; fixed `minWidth`/`width` on selects and pickers are gone and `StaffMultiSelect` takes a `style`. Tables are unchanged.
- **Mobile press feedback is one rounded highlight** - People rows, upcoming-shift rows, team rows, open-shift and request cards, swap option cards, the dashboard stat tiles and "See all" links all take the same rounded highlight that bleeds past the content, where they had a square block flush with the text, an opacity fade, or nothing at all.
- **Mobile copy** - action labels are sentence case app-wide ("Sign in", "Send invitation", "Add to schedule", "Change access level", "Open shifts"), and the login screen checks "your organization", never a workspace.

#### Fixed

- **Schedule waits for the organization's time zone; email refuses localhost links in production** - the mobile schedule fetches only once bootstrap has said which zone "today" is in, so a cold start near the UTC day boundary no longer loads the wrong week first; `emailBaseUrl()` throws in production when neither `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` nor `NEXT_PUBLIC_VERCEL_URL` is set instead of mailing localhost links.
- **Launch sweep** - mobile Requests refetches after a claim, response or volunteer so a taken shift's Claim button does not linger; the Requests tab strip no longer jumps when the last pending request is decided (deep links still win); the schedule hero keeps last night's overnight shift on duty until it ends and does not call tonight's active at 2 AM, nor a finished shift "Upcoming" when browsing an earlier day; late in a week it says "Nothing more this week" rather than nothing scheduled; the current time is read as `h23` so midnight can never render as "24:xx" and hide every same-day request; on web an approver may withdraw a request its recipient has not answered (parity with mobile and the RPC), a failed approve, reject or cancel keeps the dialog and the note, the approval note is bounded like the others, and the call-off notice hours are right across daylight-saving changes.
- **One word for taking an open shift** - a coverage gap and a posted pickup both say Claim on web and mobile; the two had said Volunteer and Claim Shift for the same act.
- **Mobile schedule: absence weeks and week paging** - a future week holding only absences leads with the first absence instead of "Nothing scheduled this week" above a list that shows it; the hero card for an absence leads with the absence's own name under the Away badge rather than restating Absence three ways; and paging to another week holds the skeleton until that week arrives instead of flashing every empty state over the previous week's data.
- **The Absences and call-offs report says when people called off** - every call-off in the range appears whatever became of it, with the shift it dropped and its time, the moment it was submitted, the notice given before the shift's start in the organization's time zone (or how long after it had begun), when and by whom it was decided, and the manager's note; the summary counts published absences, call-offs and approvals, call-offs made under 24 hours before, and the average notice.
- **The Shift requests report carries the whole request** - each row now shows both sides as the card does (shift, job, focus area and time for the requester and for the teammate), the call-off reason, when it was submitted and decided, who decided it and the manager's note, beside the type, status and time to resolution; the summary counts by outcome and type and averages the time to resolution.
- **Cancelling a request awaiting its recipient takes a note to both people** - a manager withdrawing a swap (or a pickup offered to one person) before the recipient answers can leave an optional note, the way approve and reject can; migration `029` adds the note to `cancel_shift_request`, and both the requester and the recipient are notified with it (`shift_request_cancelled`). On web the note field sits on the card beside Cancel request; on mobile the cancel opens the same note sheet as a decision. The two shift panels of a swap now sit nearly flush with the arrow disc straddling their seam, pointing from the requester's shift to the one it goes to, on web and mobile.
- **Mobile: no push-token warning on the simulator** - an iOS simulator cannot mint a push token, so it now counts as push-unsupported instead of asking `expo-notifications` and raising the library's warning toast on every dev launch.
- **Mobile dashboard with no coverage requirements** - the Coverage card showed nothing for the meter and "0 open gaps", which read as fully staffed rather than as nothing to measure. It now says coverage requirements are not configured and where to set them, keeps the pending-approvals count, and the page takes the brand wash instead of no wash.
- **Approving or rejecting on mobile opens a sheet** - the decision's optional note was a text field inside a confirmation modal, which the design rules forbid; it is now a small sheet with the same field and buttons.
- **Open-shift assignments no longer carry a custom time** - staffing an open shift as a scheduler, and volunteering for a coverage gap on the web, wrote the shift's own default hours into the cell as a custom time, so every assignment made that way showed "Custom time" and a time pill. Both flows now send no custom time unless the called-off shift itself had one (`open-shift-staffing.ts` keeps the resolved times for conflict checks only). Migration `027` adds `normalize_schedule_custom_times` inside `sync_schedule_cell_snapshot`, the one write path for every snapshot, so a custom time equal to the segment's default is stored as null whichever caller sends it, and repairs the rows already written that way.
- **Seeded shift requests** - `npm run seed` now leaves Calm Haven with four standing requests (a swap asked of the demo login, one the demo login asks, a swap awaiting approval, an open pickup), picked from the seeded schedule by `scripts/lib/seed-shift-requests.ts` and checked against the database's own `swap_request_conflict` rule before insert, so the Requests screens have content after every reset.
- **Approval re-checks a swap against today's schedule** - migration `028` keeps the original `resolve_shift_request` body under a private name and puts a thin function of the same signature in front that runs `swap_request_conflict` before delegating, so time off published after a swap was requested now refuses the approval instead of being overwritten.
- **The web Requests badge matches the board** - approvers see every active request, the number their Approval Queue tab shows (it used to count only the pending ones); everyone else sees the requests awaiting their answer.
- **Conflicting swaps are refused when made, not when approved** - migration `026` adds a `BEFORE INSERT` trigger on `shift_requests` (`swap_request_conflict`) that refuses a swap whose two shifts overlap on the same day (the identical-shift case), or where either person already works overlapping hours, or has published time off, on the day a shift would move to. Approval used to be the first place these were caught, and it never checked the time-off case at all. The web and mobile swap pickers apply the same time-off rule so the option is never offered.
- Web request cards show each shift's job, focus area and time; the default shift job stays unnamed.
- Swap partners and pickup claimants are notified when a request is finally approved or rejected, not only the requester (item 34); declined claimants are told; notifications are no longer double-sent.
- The trial welcome modal waits for onboarding to finish and mounts only on authenticated app routes (item 36); the app shell no longer remounts when permissions resolve.
- The publish banner's show-highlights control works after publishing, and the Highlight Changes toggle is honored on Month view and Mobile Day view (items 27, 31); shifts appended to an untouched published cell count as new everywhere.
- Migration `019`'s trigger raised on every publish that added a cell and `publish_schedule` mis-cast split-shift times; both repaired in `020`, and the readiness inspector fails an environment holding `019` alone.
- The web dashboard's schedule strip, hero and Your Week list badge each pill the way the grid does: a double shift with one replaced and one added shift reads Edited and New rather than Edited twice, time and mentored edits get their chip, a removed segment or a replaced absence marks the cell, and published snapshots that name a since-archived shift still resolve.
- Web header overflow at narrow widths and high zoom (account menu label, trial badge, nav row), the Alerts and Schedule toolbars at high zoom, the tablet dashboard grid, and the schedule totals-row border at fractional zoom.
- Mobile: iOS 26's native back button (react-native-screens pinned to 4.17.x, no JS back button), sticky actions above the iOS tab bar, large titles collapsing on footer screens, the iOS header shadow, skeletons under the status bar, the switch ring, link buttons shrinking their label, the request sheet's one-tap exit, in-sheet confirmations (an iOS Modal beside an open sheet never presents), skeletons redrawn to the screens they stand in for, and a skeleton audit across every screen.
- Organization suspension, billing, and archival causes are hidden from regular Users and Admins on web and mobile.
- CI on the release PR: `npm audit` allowlist entries carry reasons and review dates, the live-DB integration job resolves workspace packages, and Playwright specs pass on Linux runners.

#### Security

- See Added above: live tenant authorization (migrations `016`, `017`), sensitive-action reauthentication, single-use invitation and recovery credentials (`018`), a fail-closed CSRF policy with classified exceptions, and per-email, per-IP, and global login limits. ### Added

- **Alerts go to their subject** - tapping an alert anywhere (web bell popup, web Alerts page, mobile Alerts list) marks it read and opens what it is about: the request board, the schedule on that date, the person, People > Invitations, Settings > Subscription, or the member's own profile. One shared destination resolver serves web and mobile. The web Alerts page loses its sidebar: Inbox / Archived, the read filter, search, category, priority, sort, bulk actions and Mark all read live in one toolbar over the list. Mobile rows stop truncating, show a human note inline, and swipe in the iOS Mail idiom. Gridmasters keep a Details disclosure for platform rows.
- **One number badge** - notification, tab, open-shift and staffing counts share one pill-shaped contract on web and mobile, sized and labelled the same for one- and two-digit values.
- **Mobile Requests date rail** - the Available tab fronts each day with the same date tile as Home's Your Week, joined to the next day by a line.
- **Web number fields step** - a minus and a plus at either end of every integer field alongside the arrow keys; the mouse wheel never changes a value.

- **Date-navigated activity logs** - the organization Activity Log, the per-person Activity page, and the Gridmaster audit log are now organized around time instead of one endless scroll. A shared toolbar steps through Day, Week, or Month periods with previous, next, Today, and a jump-to-date calendar; the organization log keeps its period in the URL so a specific day can be linked and refreshed. Only the selected period loads, rows group under sticky day headings with per-day counts, and four stat boxes report the period's events, days with activity, people involved, and most common activity type. Each view carries the same one-line toolbar: previous and next around a period label that is itself the calendar for jumping to any date, the period length, a return to the current period, then search and a category filter. Each view's filter lists only the categories its rows can actually contain: a person's activity offers People, Access and roles, and Invitations, and an organization's log drops platform-only rows it can never return. Day headings and period totals come from a new count endpoint rather than the loaded page, so a busy period reports what it actually holds. Every day boundary, day label, and clock time is resolved in the organization's timezone rather than the reader's, so a manager in another zone sees the facility's day. Empty periods offer a way back to the nearest activity instead of a dead end. The three views now share one polished table, matching the People table's conventions, with rows as the control that opens the details dialog (keyboard included) and columns folding into the description on narrow screens.
- **Per-person activity timeline** - the Activity page on a staff profile now shows everything the organization has recorded about that person: profile edits, status changes, role and permission changes, removals, and the invitation lifecycle, rendered in the same table and detail dialog as the organization Activity Log. It merges `audit_log` with the `role_change_log` ledger and the `invitations` table, so role changes made outside an audited path and invitations sent from the web (which were never audited) still appear. Super admins and admins who can view employee details can open it; before this the role-history half was gridmaster-only, so org users saw an empty page. Access audit rows whose change is a permission map now list the flags that flipped ("Allowed" / "Not allowed") in every activity view instead of "Admin Permissions: Updated".
- **Reports permission** - a 26th admin permission, `canViewReports`, now gates the Reports page, its API, and its CSV/PDF exports. Before this, any Tier 2 admin saw staff hours and activity with no way to switch it off. Existing admins keep Reports: a stored permission set that never mentions the key resolves it as on, so nothing changes on the day this ships, and a super admin can now turn the "Reports" row off per admin. Super admins are unaffected.
- **Admin baseline** - an admin whose permissions were never configured (or whose stored set predates a key) now starts from `ADMIN_DEFAULT_PERMS` in `@dubgrid/authz`: edit and publish the schedule, notes and indicators, recurring shifts, and Reports, with no people management or administration. Previously such an admin resolved to the read-only user baseline. The permissions editor opens on the same set, so a save of one switch can no longer silently revoke access the resolver was granting. Stored values still win key by key in both directions.
- **Permissions editor review step** - the org-side editor now shows the same change review before saving that the Gridmaster editor already had.
- **Mobile terms-acceptance gate** — `CURRENT_TERMS_VERSION` moved to `@dubgrid/domain` so both apps gate on one constant; the mobile bootstrap payload carries `acceptedCurrentTerms` (defaulting to `true` so a stale server can't lock anyone out) and `TermsGate` blocks the authed app until it's accepted via `POST /api/mobile/v1/profile/terms`. Web enforces this by redirecting to `/accept-terms` at login, which the mobile sign-in path never hit — a mobile-only user was never re-prompted when the terms version changed.
- **Mobile error boundaries and unmatched-route screen** — `app/_layout.tsx` and `app/(tabs)/_layout.tsx` export an Expo Router `ErrorBoundary`, and `app/+not-found.tsx` handles stale deep links; both render the new `RouteErrorScreen`. A render throw previously took the whole app down with no way back, and an unknown route fell through to Expo's developer-facing "Unmatched Route" screen.
- **Mobile spacing and radius ramps** — `mobileSpacingTokens` (4-48) and `mobileRadiusTokens` in `@dubgrid/design-tokens`, surfaced as `mobileSpace` / `mobileRadius`. The web ramps are `"px"` strings React Native can't consume, so mobile screens had no scale to reach for.

### Fixed

- **Mobile sheets stay presented while their page loads** - a screen that locked scrolling for a skeleton dropped its pull-to-refresh control, and on iOS that control is the scroll view's first child, so React remounted the whole page under it: a sheet presented from that page was dismissed and presented again (or refused by UIKit and left dead). Shift Detail hit this on every first Swap, because widening the team schedule for the swap weeks put the page back into loading. The control now stays mounted and disarmed, the detail page keeps its narrow-range data while the wide range loads, and the swap week strip carries each day's teammate count under the tile so two digits no longer truncate. Dropping a shift asks before discarding once an absence reason is chosen, the same moment the swap sheet asks.
- **Schedule and dashboard toolbars stay inside narrow screens** - both render their desktop layout for a frame at phone widths, before the media query resolves, and their control rows could not wrap or shrink there, so buttons and the staff search were pushed off the side of the screen and out of reach. The rows now wrap at any width and the search compresses instead of holding a fixed floor. This also fixes the staff search overflowing a zoomed-in desktop, which is narrow in CSS pixels without being narrow enough to reach the tablet layout.
- **End-to-end typography audits no longer time out under load** - the shared Playwright login helper called `test.setTimeout(60_000)` unconditionally, which replaced rather than raised the budget and cut every caller that had already asked for more back down to 60 seconds. The route and settings-panel audits need longer than that, so they failed on slower runs while masking whatever they had not reached yet. The helper now only raises a floor.

### Security

- **Edge middleware was never running, and is now.** The monorepo refactor moved
  `middleware.ts` from repo-root `src/` to `apps/web/middleware.ts`, one level
  above the `src/` directory Next.js looks in when the app lives at
  `src/app`. Next kept emitting a `middleware-manifest.json` naming it, so
  nothing failed loudly — it simply was never invoked. Every guard it owns was
  inert for the whole life of the monorepo layout: route gating, the
  unauthenticated redirect, subdomain enforcement, the org

  and the per-request Content-Security-Policy (unauthenticated `GET /dashboard`

- **Supabase API keys migrated to publishable/secret** — the legacy `anon` / `service_role` JWTs both derived from one JWT secret, so neither could be rotated without the other. The new `sb_publishable_…` / `sb_secret_…` keys are revocable individually, and production, CI and any local admin use now hold separate secret keys. Env vars renamed to match (`SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY`, `*_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`); reads go through `apps/web/src/lib/supabase-keys` rather than sixteen scattered `process.env` lookups.
- **All 28 npm advisories cleared, including two criticals.** Most were caused by _stale_ `overrides` pins that had themselves fallen into the vulnerable ranges (axios 1.17.0, postcss 8.5.12, dompurify 3.4.11, tar, hono). `next` 16.2.10 -> 16.3.0 alone cleared nine Next.js advisories including three highs.

Added host-persistence detection, since the payload's launch agent lives under `$HOME` where scanning a checkout would never find it.
- **Removed six root-level `check_*.mjs` scripts and `deploy_migration.mjs`** — each embedded the production project's anon key, and the migration they referenced (`026_draft_schedules.sql`) has not existed since the schema was consolidated to four files.

### Fixed

- **Deleting a user who ever had a role or permission change no longer fails** - `role_change_log.target_user_id` was `NOT NULL` while its foreign key was `ON DELETE SET NULL`, so removing such an auth user tripped a not-null violation on the ledger row. Migration `014` makes the column nullable; the row stays as history with the identity dropped, as `changed_by_id` already did.
- **Admin permission gates now match the editor** - applying recurring shifts and creating, updating, or deleting shift series need Schedule edit as well as their own key at the API, as the toolbar already required; before, a recurring-only admin could fill the grid through the API alone. Writing a schedule indicator needs the indicators key as well as notes, which was previously checked only in the cell editor. Managing departments now needs the focus-areas key rather than also accepting the labels key. Staff CSV export follows the employee-details permission instead of Staff edit.
- **Permissions editor** - the Dashboard switch no longer saves a change the resolver ignores: it reads "Included with Schedule" and so on whenever an edit level grants it, from the same `VIEW_IMPLICATIONS` map authz applies. Always-on and included views are text states instead of disabled switches, Shift Requests explains where its view level comes from, Publish and the two Configuration halves are their own rows, the counter counts what is on screen, and the Gridmaster editor no longer shows an Organization Settings switch that authz forced off. `unionPermissions` and `updateDepartmentPermissions`, left over from the retired department-template model, are gone.
- **The theme no longer flips when you sign in** — the light/dark/system preference lived in per-origin storage, so the marketing site and an org subdomain each remembered their own; starting on a light landing page and signing in with a dark OS switched you to dark mid-flow. It now travels in a root-domain `dg-theme` cookie, with a `?theme=` param covering the three login links that hop between hosts (browsers accept `domain=localhost` but then refuse to send the cookie to `sub.localhost`, so the cookie alone cannot work in local development). A blocking script adopts it ahead of next-themes' own, so the right theme lands on the first paint rather than flashing. `dg-theme` is a new essential cookie, so `CONSENT_VERSION` goes 1.1 -> 1.2 on web and mobile and every user is re-prompted once.
- **The landing-page theme toggle can get back to "System"** — it was a two-state light/dark switch, so anyone who touched it was stranded on an explicit preference with no way to follow their device again. It now cycles light -> dark -> system.
- **`npm run email:build` no longer leaves a phantom diff** — react-email's own pretty-printer uses a different style than the repo's Prettier config, so every regeneration rewrote all six Supabase auth templates with ~750 lines of formatting-only churn that looked like real work. The script now runs `prettier --write` over its output, and regenerating from a clean checkout produces no diff at all.
- **Mobile: a push tap that cold-starts the app no longer loses its destination** — `usePushResponseHandler` now also reads `getLastNotificationResponseAsync()`, which is how a tap that launches a killed app is delivered; the response listener alone only fires while the app is already running.
- **Mobile: signing out no longer leaves the device receiving the previous user's pushes** — `disablePushForCurrentDevice` revokes the Expo token before the session drops, and now runs on every sign-out path (including "Sign out all devices" and the post-password-change global sign-out), not just the Profile screen's.
- **Mobile: a slow cold-start refresh no longer reads as a silent logout** — the session-restore deadline went 4s → 12s, matching the web `AuthProvider` budget raised for the same reason (`AUTH_EDGE_CASES.md` A3).
- **Mobile: switching organizations no longer flashes the previous org's data** — the org switch now clears the query cache and resets to Home rather than marking queries stale, which kept rendering the old tenant's people and schedule until each refetch landed.
- **Mobile: a hung network probe can no longer strand the app on the splash** — `NetworkStateProvider` renders nothing until its first `expo-network` probe resolves and had no timeout; it now assumes online after 2s.
- **Mobile: returning to the foreground no longer triggers a burst of refetches** — added a 30s default `staleTime` and removed the redundant AppState listener in `useTabsGate` (which also re-subscribed on every render because it depended on the whole query object).
- **Mobile: form fields and submit buttons are reachable with the keyboard up** — `Screen` sets `automaticallyAdjustKeyboardInsets`, and `ConfirmationModal` / `BottomSheetModal` wrap in `KeyboardAvoidingView`. `BottomSheetModal` also uses live window dimensions and the real bottom safe-area inset instead of a module-scope `Dimensions` snapshot and a hardcoded per-platform pad.
- **Mobile: the admin home no longer renders a blank screen** when the dashboard query resolves empty; `AddPersonScreen` shows a skeleton instead of an empty picker beside a live validation error; the privacy screen's analytics switch no longer flashes off-then-on and surfaces save failures instead of swallowing them.

- **Mobile: legal links follow the configured API host** instead of hardcoding `app.dubgrid.com`.
- **Mobile: an open shift's time range is consistent across tabs** — the Schedule and Requests tabs each carried a private copy of the open-shift presentation accessors, and they had drifted: only the Requests copy fell back to the shift's custom times, so the same open shift showed a time range on one tab and nothing on the other. Both now share `features/schedule/lib/openShiftPresentation.ts`.
- **Mobile: declining updated terms is possible** — the terms sheet covers the entire app, so it now offers Sign out alongside Accept; previously a user who would not accept had no way out of the app at all.

---
title: "Coverage and Open Shifts"
description: "See where staffing falls short, fill an open shift, or offer to cover one."
---

Coverage compares the people scheduled for a shift and job with the minimum your Organization set. An open shift is a specific opportunity someone can fill. They are related, but they are not the same thing: a coverage gap can identify a shortfall, while an open shift can be assigned or claimed.

## Reading coverage

On the web schedule, every focus-area section has count rows. They show how many people are scheduled against how many are needed.

## Schedule Builder

Select **Schedule Builder** on the schedule toolbar to see every coverage requirement for the period at once, and, if you can edit shifts, to build the draft. The button's number is how many shifts are short. The Schedule Builder is available in **1 Week** and **2 Weeks**, to people who can see dashboard analytics.

### Building the draft

People who edit shifts see **Build the draft** at the top, for the period on screen and the focus areas the filter shows. A build starts from each person's recurring schedule: it fills the empty cells a recurring schedule covers, the same cells **Fill from Recurring Schedules** fills, including recurring absences. Then it puts back shifts the draft removed or moved away, and fills short shifts with people who are qualified and free at that time. Nobody is booked twice, and someone with a recurring absence is not scheduled that day. With a focus area filter, only people in the focus areas shown get recurring fills. It never moves or replaces anyone already scheduled, never overrides an absence or a waiting request, and never approves a pickup.

Select **Preview build** to see what it would do before anything changes: how many changes for how many people, how many shifts and absences come from recurring schedules, what kind the rest are (schedules, restores, moves back), how many shifts are short now and after, and how many people are still needed now and after, so a build that adds people without filling a shift completely still shows its progress. Select **Build** to apply it, or **Cancel**. If the draft changes after the preview, the preview is cleared; if it changed while the preview was open, **Build** shows the new preview instead of applying.

A build changes the draft only, as one line under **Applied**. **Undo** puts back every cell the build changed, except a cell someone has changed since, which keeps that change; Undo says how many it left alone.

### The matrix

Each row is one requirement, such as Day Shift for a job in a focus area, and each column is a day. A cell reads staffed / needed, for example **3/5**, and is marked:

- **Short** when fewer people are scheduled than needed.
- **Over** when more are scheduled than needed.
- **Staffed** when the numbers match.

A mentored person counts at your Organization's mentored credit, so a cell can read 2.5/3. The **Staff short** row adds up each day's shortfall. Shifts that have already started are faded and not counted. Filter by focus area to check one area at a time.

People who edit shifts see the draft. A line at the top compares it with the published schedule: how many gaps the draft opens, how many it closes, and how many other cells change. **Changes since publish** marks those cells in the matrix. Dates that have never been published have nothing to compare. People who cannot edit shifts see published dates only.

Select a cell to see it in detail:

- **Before** is what staff see now, and **After** is the draft.
- **Still needed** lists the jobs that are short.
- **What changed** lists, for people who edit shifts, each person whose draft change moved the cell's count, for example a shift moved to another day, taken off, or replaced by an absence. The lines always add up to the difference between Before and After. A cell the draft did not touch says so.
- **Fill a spot** opens the list of qualified people for that gap, the same as selecting the open shift. If pickup requests are already waiting for the shift, the Schedule Builder says how many.
- **Show in grid** closes the Schedule Builder and outlines that day in the focus area's section.
- **Ways to fix** lists the suggestions for that day, and **Not suggested** says why each other qualified person was left out, for example an absence or a shift that overlaps.

### Suggestions

People who edit shifts see a dot under each date that has a suggestion. Select a date to see that day's suggestions, best first; select it again to close them. Each suggestion is measured on a copy of the schedule first, so it never fixes one gap by opening another, and it reads complete on its own:

- who, which shift in which focus area, and the date (both dates for a move);
- why it is suggested;
- what it fixes, for example "Closes 1 gap" or "Fills 1 spot", and each requirement it changes with its count now and after;
- what applying it does: a draft change staff see after you publish, or, for an approval, a change published right away.

- **Move back** returns a shift this draft moved to another day or changed to other work.
- **Restore** puts back a shift this draft removed, when no absence or request explains the removal.
- **Schedule** adds someone who is qualified for the shift and free at that time: no absence, and no shift that overlaps it.
- **Approve** approves a pickup request already waiting for the shift. The Schedule Builder asks first, because an approval takes effect for staff right away, without a publish, and replaces any draft change of yours for that person's day.

Move back, Restore and Schedule change only the draft, the same way an edit in the grid does, and appear under **Applied** above the matrix with **Undo**. When nothing can fix a requirement that is short on the same day every week, the Schedule Builder says so.

**Apply all for this day** applies every draft fix for the selected date. Like a build, it applies them one at a time and counts again after each, so no spot is filled twice and no one is given two shifts that overlap. Approvals are never included; approve each one yourself. The whole batch appears as one line under **Applied**, and **Undo** puts it all back.

Focus areas are listed in the same order as the schedule grid.

**All requirements met** means no requirement is short or over for the period. If your Organization has not set any requirements, the Schedule Builder says so and points to [Coverage settings](/configuration/coverage-requirements).

Your Super Admin controls the requirements in [Coverage settings](/configuration/coverage-requirements). They also choose whether staff see coverage shortages and call-off vacancies in [Schedule Rules](/configuration/schedule-rules-and-display).

## Filling an open shift

If you can edit shifts, select an entry in an **Open Shifts** row. DubGrid lists people who are qualified for that shift's focus area, role, and certification and who do not have an absence or another overlapping shift that day. Choose a person to add the shift as a draft, then [publish](/features/draft-publish) it when ready.

Staff can see only the open shifts they are eligible to work when their Organization allows it. Select one to offer to cover it. The offer becomes a pickup request and needs approval before it changes the published schedule. See [Shift requests](/features/shift-requests).

## Call-off vacancies

When an approved call-off leaves a shift uncovered, it appears as an open shift if your Organization allows staff to see call-off vacancies. A staff member can offer to cover it, and a scheduler can also fill it through the normal draft-and-publish workflow.

## What affects eligibility

Your Organization's focus areas, roles, certifications, shifts, jobs, and absence types determine which people and assignments are available. A change to any of these can change who DubGrid lists for an open shift.

---
title: "Recurring Shifts"
description: "Give people a regular weekly pattern, repeat a single shift, and fill the schedule from them."
---

DubGrid has two ways to schedule shifts that repeat:

- A **recurring schedule** is a person's regular week: which shift they work on each day from Sunday to Saturday. It doesn't change the schedule until you apply it.
- A **repeating shift** is one shift or off day set to repeat on the schedule itself, daily, weekly, or every two weeks.

## Recurring schedules

Open **People** and select the **Recurring Shifts** tab. The grid shows each person's regular week, Sunday to Saturday. A person's own recurring schedule also appears on their profile under **Recurring schedule**.

If you can manage recurring schedules, change the cells, then:

- **Save Draft** keeps your changes to finish later.
- **Save** applies them after you confirm **Save Recurring Schedule Changes?**.
- **Discard** throws away the changes you haven't saved.

## Filling the schedule from recurring schedules

On the **Schedule** tab, open **Tools** and select **Auto Fill**. DubGrid counts the empty slots in the period on screen that recurring schedules would fill and asks **Auto Fill Shifts?**. Select **Fill**.

Auto Fill only fills empty slots: it never overwrites a shift already on the schedule. Everything it adds is a draft, so review it and [publish](/features/draft-publish) when you're ready.

Auto Fill leaves a cell empty when it already has a visible entry, the recurring pattern is outside its effective date range, or it refers to an archived shift, job, or absence type. Review the resulting coverage gaps instead of assuming every recurring pattern was applied.

## Repeating shifts

Select a cell on the schedule, choose the shift, and open the panel's **Repeating** section:

| Field            | Options                                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Frequency**    | Daily, Weekly, or Biweekly                         |
| **Days of Week** | The days it repeats on, for weekly and biweekly    |
| **Start Date**   | The first day                                      |
| **Ends**         | Never, On a date, or After a number of occurrences |

A repeating shift runs for at most 183 occurrences, about six months. Its shifts are drafts until published.

If some of its days already have a shift or off day, DubGrid asks before creating it. **Replace** puts the repeating shift on those days too. **Keep existing** leaves them as they are, and those days are not part of the series.

To change or remove a repeating shift, select one of its shifts. Under **Repeating shift: edit scope**, choose what the change applies to:

| Scope                | Applies to                                     |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **This shift**       | Only the shift you selected                    |
| **This and earlier** | That shift and every earlier one in the series |
| **This and later**   | That shift and every later one in the series   |
| **All in series**    | Every shift in the series                      |

Then change or remove the shift and select **Confirm**. Removed shifts are marked for deletion and come off the schedule when you publish. Removing **This and later** ends the series the day before, and removing every shift ends the series.

Changing or removing a series affects only its own shifts, never a day you kept when you created it.

## Who can do what

| To                                | You need                                                   |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| See recurring schedules           | Permission to view recurring shifts                        |
| Change recurring schedules        | Permission to manage recurring shifts                      |
| Use Auto Fill                     | Permission to edit shifts and to apply recurring schedules |
| Create or delete repeating shifts | Permission to edit shifts and to manage shift series       |

Super Admins can do all of these. New Admins have them on by default, and their Super Admin can change that per person. See [Roles and permissions](/reference/roles).

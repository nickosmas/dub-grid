---
title: "Recurring Shifts"
description: "Give people a regular weekly pattern, repeat a single shift, and fill the schedule from them."
---

DubGrid has two ways to schedule shifts that repeat:

- A **recurring schedule** is a person's regular week: which shift they work on each day from Sunday to Saturday. It doesn't change the schedule until you apply it.
- A **repeating shift** is one shift or absence set to repeat on the schedule itself, daily, weekly, or every two weeks.

## Recurring schedules

Open **People** and select the **Recurring Shifts** tab. The grid shows each person's regular week, Sunday to Saturday. A person's own recurring schedule also appears on their profile under **Recurring schedule**.

If you can manage recurring schedules, change the cells, then:

- **Save Draft** keeps your changes to finish later.
- **Save** applies them after you confirm **Save Recurring Schedule Changes?**.
- **Discard** throws away the changes you haven't saved.

## Filling the schedule from recurring schedules

On the **Schedule** tab, open **Tools** and select **Fill from Recurring Schedules**. DubGrid counts the empty slots in the period on screen that recurring schedules would fill and asks **Fill from Recurring Schedules?**. Select **Fill**.

It only fills empty slots: it never overwrites a shift already on the schedule. Everything it adds is a draft, so review it and [publish](/features/draft-publish) when you're ready.

It leaves a cell empty when it already has a visible entry, the recurring pattern is outside its effective date range, or it refers to an archived shift, job, or absence type. Review the resulting coverage gaps instead of assuming every recurring pattern was applied.

The [Schedule Builder](/features/coverage-and-open-shifts#auto-build) starts every build the same way, filling empty cells from recurring schedules by the same rules, before it fills the shifts still short.

## Preferences

On **People > Recurring Shifts**, the **Preferences** column shows each person's shift and job preferences. Select it to open the dialog, then choose **Shifts** or **Jobs**; each shows its current setting. **Back** returns to that first step and keeps what you chose, and **Save** saves both.

- **Any shift** or **Any job**: Auto-build can give them any shift or job they're qualified for.
- **Prefers some shifts** or **Prefers some jobs**: they get the ones you tick first, and others when needed.
- **Only some shifts** or **Only some jobs**: Auto-build and its suggestions never give them any other, for example someone who only supervises.

Each focus area has its own shifts, so the dialog lists only the shifts of the person's focus areas (and any shift for every focus area), with their times, grouped by focus area. With Only, a focus area where you choose no shift means they get no shifts there, and the dialog says so. The jobs listed are the ones they're qualified for in their focus areas. Choose at least one for Prefers or Only. Both apply together: someone with Only Supervisor and Prefers Day Shift gets only Supervisor work, Day shifts first. The column then reads, for example, "Only Supervisor · Prefers Day Shift". A preference saves straight away and does not change their recurring schedule, which recurring fills still follow.

## Repeating shifts

Select a cell on the schedule, choose the shift, and open the panel's **Repeating** section:

| Field            | Options                                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Frequency**    | Daily, Weekly, or Biweekly                         |
| **Days of Week** | The days it repeats on, for weekly and biweekly    |
| **Start Date**   | The first day                                      |
| **Ends**         | Never, On a date, or After a number of occurrences |

A repeating shift runs for at most 183 occurrences, about six months. Its shifts are drafts until published.

If some of its days already have a shift or absence, DubGrid asks before creating it. **Replace** puts the repeating shift on those days too. **Keep existing** leaves them as they are, and those days are not part of the series.

To change or remove a repeating shift, select one of its shifts. Under **Repeating shift: edit scope**, choose what the change applies to:

| Scope                | Applies to                                     |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **This shift**       | Only the shift you selected                    |
| **This and earlier** | That shift and every earlier one in the series |
| **This and later**   | That shift and every later one in the series   |
| **All in series**    | Every shift in the series                      |

Then change or remove the shift and select **Confirm**. Removed shifts are marked for deletion and come off the schedule when you publish. Removing **This and later** ends the series the day before, and removing every shift ends the series.

Changing or removing a series this way affects only its own shifts, never a day you kept when you created it.

To change when a repeating shift happens, select one of its shifts and choose **Edit dates and pattern**. The same fields open with the series' current values, and the summary says how many days the change adds and removes. Save or discard any change to the selected day first.

Under **Days already in the series**, choose what happens to days that stay in the pattern:

- **Keep their changes**: a day you changed on its own keeps that change.
- **Reset to match**: every day goes back to the repeating shift.

Days that leave the pattern are removed the same way as above. If a day joining it already has a shift or absence, DubGrid asks first, as when creating one. Select **Save Repeating Shift** to apply it.

## Who can do what

| To                                | You need                                                   |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| See recurring schedules           | Permission to view recurring shifts                        |
| Change recurring schedules        | Permission to manage recurring shifts                      |
| Use Fill from Recurring Schedules | Permission to edit shifts and to apply recurring schedules |
| Create or delete repeating shifts | Permission to edit shifts and to manage shift series       |

Super Admins can do all of these. New Admins have them on by default, and their Super Admin can change that per person. See [Roles and permissions](/reference/roles).

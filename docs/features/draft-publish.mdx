---
title: "Draft and Publish"
description: "Schedule changes stay drafts until someone publishes them. How drafts, publishing, and discarding work."
---

Every change to the schedule starts as a draft. Staff keep seeing the published schedule until someone allowed to publish makes the drafts live, so nobody sees a half-finished schedule.

## Drafts

Only people who can edit the schedule see drafts. A changed cell is marked **Draft new**, **Draft changes**, or **Marked for deletion**. Drafts are saved as you make them, so closing the browser doesn't lose them, and every editor sees everyone's drafts.

While there are drafts in the period you're looking at, a **Draft changes** banner shows how many shifts are new, edited, or deleted and how many schedule notes changed. If other periods have drafts too, a second banner, such as **Drafts in 3 other weeks** (or pay periods, or months), takes you to them: each period is a button with its dates and number of drafts, such as **10/25 - 10/31 (16)**. A banner shows at most two buttons, so with more periods the nearest keeps its button and the rest are listed under **2 more**.

## Publishing

1. Select **Publish** in the banner. If you can't publish, the button explains why.
2. The **Publish Schedule?** dialog lists the changes in this period by who made them: **Your drafts** first, then each other editor's. New shifts and new absences, such as a day off, are counted separately. It warns you if an editor is on the schedule right now, since anything they haven't saved yet won't be included, and if coverage gaps remain. If your Organization has coverage requirements, it also says how many gaps this publish opens and closes compared with what staff see now. It also lists everyone the publish leaves with more than 5 days in a row (counting the weeks and pay periods on either side), more than 5 days in a Sunday-to-Saturday week, or more than 40 hours in that week, such as "Laura Marshall: 6 days in a row, Wed, Nov 25 to Mon, Nov 30". It's a warning only: you can still publish.
3. Select **Publish**.

Publishing covers the period on screen. Drafts in other periods stay drafts until you publish those too.

After publishing, everyone viewing the schedule sees the change. Each person whose shifts changed (except you) gets one alert listing every changed day with what it was before and what it is now (a shift on a day they had nothing reads as new), as a push notification on their phone and, if they turned on schedule emails, an email. If your organization hides what changed from staff, their alert names the days only. Nobody else is alerted. A **Recently published** bar shows what changed in the period you are viewing, and another counts changes published in other periods. Someone who can't edit or publish the schedule sees only the days their own shifts changed, such as "Your shifts changed on Tue, Nov 3", and no bar when none of theirs did.

In the desktop one-week and two-week grids, **Highlight Changes** marks new, changed, deleted shifts and changed schedule notes until you hide them. The month view and phone-sized web view do not show this overlay, so switch to a desktop week view when you need to review individual changed cells. Hover a changed shift to see what it was before this period's first publish in view, with its job, times, hours and schedule area; a day with two shifts or an absence shows the whole day before and after. A shift on a day that had nothing shows as added, with no before. Selecting a highlighted shift opens it with the same marks, when it was published, who published it when your organization shows that, and what the day was before if a shift it had was changed or removed.

A period's first publication has nothing published before it to compare with, so when you come back to the schedule later its shifts aren't highlighted; later changes to that period are. Right after you publish, everything you've published since you last opened the schedule is highlighted, a period's first publication included.

Super Admins can always publish. Admins can publish when their Super Admin allows it, which is on for new Admins by default.

## Discarding drafts

Select **Discard** in the banner. The **Discard drafts?** dialog offers **Discard my drafts**, which removes only your own drafts in this period, or **Keep drafts**. When other editors also have drafts, a Super Admin also sees **Discard all drafts**.

Discarding leaves the published schedule as it is and doesn't touch drafts in other periods. It also keeps what marking someone away changed on their days, which the dialog says; to undo those, change the person's status. Discarded drafts can't be restored.

## What staff see

Staff see only the published schedule. A period that has never been published shows **This period has not been published yet**. Your organization chooses whether staff also see what each changed shift was before and who published it; see [Schedule rules and display](/configuration/schedule-rules-and-display#what-staff-see-after-a-publish). Approved shift requests are different: they change the published schedule straight away, without a draft. See [Shift requests](/features/shift-requests).
